=====================================================================
FILE 02 — VISUAL POLISH & MICRO-DETAILS
Answers Dossier Question 1: "Where do shapes still look like basic
boxes? What micro-details are missing?"
=====================================================================

THE DIAGNOSIS
-------------
Your large forms are now excellent (spline skeleton tunnel, catenary
bridges, open-hatch treehouse). What still reads "procedural" is the
ABSENCE of small things. Real hand-drawn pages are DENSE with tiny
marks; your forest floor between props is empty paper. The fix is
not new mega-prefabs — it is a micro-detail kit and a placement
discipline.

THE CORE PRINCIPLE — THREE READS
--------------------------------
Every prop must survive three inspection distances:
  40m (silhouette read): distinct outline against sky/fog
  10m (shape read):      recognizable form + correct scale
  2m  (texture read):    surface marks — hatch bands, moss, litter
Your trees pass 40m and 10m. Most fail 2m: the titan trunk is a bare
cylinder, boulders have no base debris, the ground has no litter.
Players feel this as "something is fake" without knowing why.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 1 — GROUND LITTER (biggest win per line of code)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The forest bed is ~11,000 m² of flat green with 4 trail decals.
Real forest floors are 60% litter. Build these four tiny builders
(all noCollide, all via addGeo so they merge into ink buckets):

  buildLeafLitter(x, z): 3-7 tiny flat boxes, 0.15-0.4 long, GREEN
    and BLACK, rotated random, y = 0.015-0.03, slight tilt.
  buildTwig(x, z): thin brown-black box 0.5-1.2 long, 0.04 thick,
    sometimes 2 segments with a 20-40° kink (2 boxes).
  buildPebble(x, z): small facetedRock scale 0.15-0.3, BLACK.
  buildPinecone(x, z): tiny cone, ORANGE/BROWN, under conifers only.

DISCIPLINE — scatter rules (Dream will automate this, File 08):
  - Litter density is highest at sector edges and prop bases,
    lowest on trails and the central clearing (trails swept clean
    = they READ as trails even harder).
  - Under every tree: 5-10 litter pieces + 1-2 twigs inside a
    2.5m radius. Under every boulder: pebbles at the base ring.
  - Along trail edges (not on them): grass tufts every 3-4m —
    buildGrassClump already exists; just call it along the trail
    splines with a 1.2-2m side offset.

Cost check: ~1,500 litter pieces across the map ≈ merged geometry
in your ink buckets — near-zero draw cost since addGeo merges.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 2 — BARK & SURFACE MARKS (the 2m read for the hero pieces)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- TITAN TRUNK CROSSHATCH (concept promises "deeply crosshatched
  black ironwood bark" — the trunk is currently a bare 12-segment
  cylinder). Add 8-12 shallow noCollide band boxes wrapped at
  varying heights and angles around the trunk: thin (0.08-0.15)
  BLACK boxes slightly proud of the surface, alternate ±15° tilt.
  At 2m this reads as crosshatch; at 40m invisible. ~20 boxes.
- HORIZONTAL BARK RINGS on the redwood every 2-3m of height
  (redwoods have furrowed horizontal bands) — same trick, rings
  instead of vertical strips. buildPineTree already does vertical
  ridge strips — good — add 2 more strips (3 is sparse).
- ROOT FLARES at every significant tree base: 4-6 low wedges
  radiating out (you already do this in buildAncientTree — reuse
  the same 6 lines for buildPineTree and the titan trunk, scaled).
  Roots ground a tree like nothing else. Highest-value 20 boxes
  in the whole map.
- MOSS PATCHES: thin GREEN flat boxes (0.6-1.4, y+0.02) on the
  NORTH side of trunks and boulders (moss grows shaded-north in
  the real world — players who know will smile; it also subtly
  teaches navigation).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 3 — CANOPY UNDERSIDE & HANGING LIFE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Looking UP in your forest: cones and spheres from outside. Looking
up FROM below is currently empty between skirts.
- UNDERSIDE FOLIAGE DISC: a flat GREEN polygon/box cluster just
  under each conifer skirt tier and under the banyan crown —
  so vertical fights see leaf-cover overhead, and it doubles as
  real shade geometry.
- HANGING MOSS STRANDS: 0.03-thin GREEN boxes, 0.5-1.2 long,
  hanging from rope-bridge ropes, banyan limbs, and perimeter
  eaves. 2-4 per bridge. They also sell "old and heavy".
- VINES on the banyan: 3-5 vertical thin cylinders from lower
  limbs to ground with a slight lean — bonus: they read as soft
  cover even if noCollide.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 4 — FAKE BLOB SHADOWS (single biggest "grounded" upgrade)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Your world has NO shadows (ink pipeline). Objects float slightly
optically. Hand-drawn art solves this with a scribble shadow:
- Under every significant prop (trees, boulders, bridges ends,
  cabin, cordwood): a flat ellipse of BLACK ink at y = 0.02-0.03,
  noCollide, using your fill=1 mode at ~25-35% coverage — your
  hatch system treats fill as solid ink; a THIN ellipse will read
  as a hatched shadow patch.
- Implementation: one buildBlobShadow(x, z, rx, rz, inkOpacity)
  emitting a flat box or ellipse geometry into the merged bucket.
- Offset it 0.3-0.6m opposite your LIGHT_WORLD direction so the
  whole map's shadows agree with your light — instant coherence.
- Cost: near zero (merged decal geometry). Impact: enormous —
  this is the #1 "why does it look expensive" trick for a
  shadowless renderer.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 5 — SKY & PAPER WORLD (the frame around the diorama)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Your fog-to-paper fade is great. Now populate the paper:
- PAPER CLOUDS: 4-7 flat white blobs high up (fill=1, WHITE-ish
  ink via a 7th ink slot or paper-colored), with one BLACK outline
  edge box each — classic notebook clouds. Slow drift: 0.2 m/s
  (File 05 shows the animation hook).
- BIRO SUN: one ORANGE disc + 8 short radiating strokes, fixed
  opposite LIGHT_WORLD so light direction is diegetic.
- DISTANT BIRDS: 3-5 tiny black "V" chevrons (two thin boxes each)
  drifting in a slow loop at Y 25-30 — motion in the sky sells
  life more than any ground detail.
- MARGIN DOODLES: outside the play bounds, a couple of giant faint
  sketch doodles (a tic-tac-toe grid, a spiral, "LVL 1" scrawl)
  drawn as noCollide BLUE thin boxes on the paper — visible only
  when you grapple high. Reward for looking around.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 6 — WATER & BANK DETAILS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- RIPPLE RINGS: 3-4 thin BLUE ellipse outlines (flat boxes) around
  stepping stones, and one at the trout landing point (sync with
  the fish timer — spawn ring when the fish lands, File 05).
- REEDS: 5-8 thin GREEN cylinders, 0.6-1.1 tall, clustered at 6-8
  bank spots, slight random lean.
- LILY PADS: 3-5 flat GREEN small discs near the south reach.
- STONE WET-LINE: a 0.15-tall BLACK band box at each stepping
  stone's waterline (stones look "in" the water, not "on" it).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 7 — THE CONIFER SKYLINE, DONE PROPERLY RAGGED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Replace the every-12m loop with clustered blue-noise:
  - Two depth rows: row A at ±(P-4), row B at ±(P-10), row B only
    ~60% density and taller (24-28m) — depth = majesty.
  - Heights clustered: use 3 height bands (20/24/28) with runs of
    2-4 same-band neighbors — skylines cluster in real forests.
  - Leave 2-3 deliberate GAPS where distant paper-fog shows
    through (a gap is a landmark; "the notch" becomes nav language).
  - One BROKEN/leaning giant near a corner (lean 8-12°, no collapse)
    — every real forest has one; players will use it as a beacon.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
KIT 8 — LANTERNS IN THE BAMBOO (already specced, never built)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Concept 4.2 promises overhead paper lanterns at Y=9. Build:
  buildBambooLantern(x, z, y): horizontal pole box between two
  culms + hanging 0.35 cube lantern (paper fill=1, warm ORANGE
  outline) + short drop rope. 5-7 lanterns along the grove's
  diagonal. They are grapple anchors (so the grove gets the aerial
  layer it currently lacks) AND a beautiful night-light story for
  File 06's fire-glow shader.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DETAIL-RADIUS RULE (budget your effort like real studios)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Spend micro-detail budget by player-dwell probability:
  - Radius 0-8m around LANDMARKS (treehouse, skull portal, campfire,
    altar): 3x detail density. Players congregate here.
  - Along trails: 1.5x (eyes move fast but always see it).
  - Sector mid-zones: 1x.
  - Above Y=12 (flyover-only zones): silhouette only, skip litter.
Make it a multiplier parameter in your scatter function NOW so
Dream inherits it (File 07): density = base * detailRadius(x, z).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT NOT TO DO
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Do not add detail by raising poly counts on big shapes — your
  style lives on flat inked planes; keep silhouettes clean.
- Do not scatter uniformly. Uniform scatter is the "procedural"
  smell. Cluster + clear.
- Do not detail what the fog already hides (14m-110m fade covers
  most of the perimeter interior — detail the inner 30m first).
