=====================================================================
FILE 03 — BIOME COHESION & TRANSITIONS
Answers Dossier Question 2: "How can the transition between the
bamboo plantation, redwood treehouse, and mountain stream feel more
natural and continuous rather than compartmentalized quadrants?"
=====================================================================

THE DIAGNOSIS
-------------
Your map is four sectors placed at four fixed centers with props
INSIDE their rectangles, on one uniform green floor. Nothing in the
GROUND or the SKY says where one region ends and the next begins —
so the eye reads "four islands in a green box" the moment you stop
looking at the hero pieces. The props are great; the FIELD between
them is empty. Real biomes are FIELDS (density fades continuously),
not RECTANGLES (density steps at a border).

The fix has 6 techniques. All of them become Dream rules in File 07/
08 — here you apply them by hand to the forest, but write the code
as generic functions from day one.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 1 — SECTOR INFLUENCE FIELDS (kill the hard border)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Every sector stops being a rectangle and becomes a FIELD:
  influence(x, z) = smoothstep(R_outer, R_inner, dist(x, z, center))
  - R_inner = the tight zone (full density)
  - R_outer = the fade edge (zero density), typically 1.4x R_inner
Any scatter/prop placement multiplies its probability by the summed
influence of its sector. Result: bamboo culms thin out gradually
into scattered individual stalks between the grove and the pine
grove — exactly how real vegetation behaves.

Code seed (put it in a shared module NOW, Dream reuses it):
  function makeField(cx, cz, rIn, rOut) {
    return (x, z) => {
      const d = Math.hypot(x - cx, z - cz);
      return 1 - smoothstep(rIn, rOut, d);   // 1 inside, 0 outside
    };
  }
  // combined weight at a point:
  const w = fields.reduce((acc, f) => acc + f(x, z), 0);

Forest application:
  bamboo  field: center (-28,-26), rIn 9,  rOut 15
  pines   field: center (34,-26),  rIn 8,  rOut 13
  skeleton: center (-24, 8), rIn 10, rOut 14 (elongated: use
           an elliptical distance — dist to segment, not point)
  glade  field: center (23, 27),  rIn 8,  rOut 12
  river  field: ribbon along x ≈ 21, half-width 4->7 (dist |x-21|)

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 2 — GROUND-TINT WASHES (paint the floor like a mapmaker)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The single loudest "box" signal is the uniform green floor. Real
mapmakers wash the paper: dirt around the campfire, paler moss near
the river, darker green under dense canopy.
- Add 8-12 large, thin, noCollide ellipse decals (y 0.008-0.015,
  layered under trails) in 3 tints:
    beaten earth (OR) — extends each sector's heart beyond trails
    dark moss   (GR, deeper green box under existing floor? no —
                 use a slightly DARKER green thin decal above floor)
    silt/pale   (BLUE-GRAY wash along both riverbanks, 2-3m wide)
- Rules: washes overlap each other freely; their edges are never
  circles — superellipse or 2 overlapping offset ellipses each.
- The campfire gets a charred ring (BLACK wash + the ember bed).
- This is data, not code per map: each sector spec gains
    groundTint: { ink, rx, rz, offset } (File 07).
Cost: ~12 merged decals. Visual win: the floor stops being a box.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 3 — TRANSITION BELTS (stage the handshake explicitly)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Where two fields overlap (influence of both > 0.25), place BLEND
PROPS — things that legitimately belong to both sides:
  - NW bamboo x N pines belt: 3-4 lone pine trees amid the outermost
    bamboo culms + fern clusters + a fallen bamboo culm lying on the
    ground (a 3-segment kinked thin cylinder chain — gorgeous
    10-minute build, huge believability).
  - Pines x river belt: boulders getting progressively rounder/mossy
    toward the water + reeds (File 02 Kit 6) + a fallen log bridging
    a bank gap.
  - Skeleton x glade belt: bone shards half-buried (small OR wedge
    tips poking from the ground, tilted 30-50°) + cordwood.
  - Glade x river belt: the logging cart + plank offcuts + sawdust
    wash (pale OR decal).
Rule of thumb: 4-8 blend props per shared border, placed IN the
overlap band (not inside either heart).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 4 — CANOPY CONTINUITY (unify the sky)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
From the deck at Y=9.5 the player sees sector roofs. If canopies
don't overlap, sectors read as separate dioramas from above.
- Add 4-6 "connector canopy" clusters: 2-3 mid-height trees
  (14-18m, slender archetype) planted IN the belts between sectors,
  crowns overlapping both sides.
- Raise 2-3 existing giant grass tufts near belt edges to 9-10m
  with drooping apex fronds — cheap "bridge" silhouettes.
- The trail-side conifers (currently 4 lonely interior pines at
  ±40/±16) are good — add 6-8 more at half-density along the N and
  W belts so the tree line continues between regions.
Rule: from the treehouse deck, the horizon should be a CONTINUOUS
ragged tree line in every direction — no green-floor gaps.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 5 — TRAILS THAT STORYTELL (routing, not just decals)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Your trails currently skirt sector edges (the west trail passes 14m
south of the bamboo grove it claims to lead to). Trails should
thread the sector hearts — they are the guided tour:
  - West trail: add nodes clipping the grove's south edge
      [-50,0], [-42,-2], [-33,-8], [-27,-14] (enters bamboo),
      then exits [-18,-8], [-12,-2], [-7,0]
  - North trail: jog it through the pine grove's west edge:
      [0,-50], [-2,-44], [6,-38], [10,-32] (clips NE sector),
      [-2,-24], [0,-16], [0,-7]
  - South trail already passes the campfire heart — good.
  - East trail: fixed in File 01 Finding 7 (bridge routing).
Plus trail FURNITURE every ~12m (alternate sides, never blocking
the 1.8m channel): flat stone (stepping-stone reuse), trail sign
(exists — add 2 more at sector boundaries pointing sector-to-
sector; hand-drawn arrows = diegetic wayfinding), fern cluster,
leaf-litter pocket (Kit 1 density bump).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
TECHNIQUE 6 — LANDMARK SIGHTLINE BEACONS (cohesion through views)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Disorientation is what makes sectors feel disconnected. Give every
sector ONE guaranteed sightline to the center crown and ONE to a
neighboring sector's silhouette:
  - From bamboo heart: see the titan crown over the culm tips
    (culms 8-14m at rIn 9 — crown at 28m: ensure one south-facing
    culm gap corridor, then VERIFY with a raycast in your verifier,
    File 09).
  - From skeleton tunnel mouth (north portal): align the skull's
    hollow eyes to frame the treehouse trunk 32m away. Free drama.
  - From pine grove: a pinned-back curtain view toward the east
    bridge (fell 1 perimeter conifer, leave the stump + a "chopped"
    wedge notch — environmental storytelling AND a window).
  - From glade: sightline up the south trail to the south bridge.
Implementation: after build, run 4 raycasts from sector hearts to
the 4 beacon targets; log PASS/FAIL. (File 09 turns this into an
automated invariant — "every sector sees the crown".)

---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SECTOR SPEC (bridge to the Dream system)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Encode everything above as data — this exact shape is what File 07
blows up into full map recipes:
  sectors: [
    { id: 'bamboo', center: [-28,-26], rIn: 9, rOut: 15,
      palette: 'grove', core: 'bamboo_plantation',
      props: [ {id:'bamboo_lantern', n:6, placement:'ridge'},
               {id:'mossy_altar', n:1, placement:'heart'} ],
      tint: { ink:'OR', rx:10, rz:9 },
      blendWith: [
        { other:'pines', props:['lone_pine','fallen_culm','fern'] } ],
      beacon: { target:'crown', mustSee:true } },
    ...
  ]
Nothing here is hardcoded geometry — it is a recipe any biome can
eat. Pirate cove: same spec, palette 'tropical', core 'shipwreck'.
