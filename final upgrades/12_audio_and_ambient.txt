=====================================================================
FILE 12 — AUDIO & AMBIENCE ON A ZERO BUDGET
The notebook world is silent. Here is its voice — cheap, legal, and
spatialized.
=====================================================================

WHY AUDIO IS THE CHEAPEST WOW LEFT
----------------------------------
Visuals: you compete with everyone. Sound: most browser games ship
SILENT. A pencil-scratch footstep layer alone puts Doodle Strike in
the top 5% of web game feel. Budget: one afternoon + 0 dollars.

THE SOUND IDENTITY (decide it before downloading anything)
----------------------------------------------------------
Everything is MADE OF PAPER AND INK, so every sound is:
  - dry and close (paper, pencil, cardboard — no reverb tails),
  - small (the player is a doodle miniature; events are tiny),
  - handwritten (wobbly, imperfect — layer a human vocal "skrch"
    under synth sounds rather than polishing them out).
Consistency law: if a sound wouldn't exist on a desk (metal ping,
deep explosions), pass it through the paper filter of this identity
or replace it: explosion = paper CRUMPLE + low thump, not Hollywood.

THE SOUND PALETTE (what maps to what)
-------------------------------------
  footsteps     short pencil strokes; per surface: grass = soft
                double-scratch, dirt/trail = dry single tap, wood
                (decks/bridges) = light knock + creak every 4th
                step, water/stepping stones = plip + tiny splat
  fire/heartbeat of the map: crackle loop (paper crinkle, granular)
  grapple fire  pen CLICK + zip = biro spring + fast scratch sweep
  landing       soft THUD = palm on desk (record your own!)
  hit confirm   short pencil tick + tiny splat
  kill          pencil SCRIBE (a fast circle) + tear snippet
  stalker laser thin sustained pen-roll while aiming (see File 11)
  player hurt   one page RIP + muffled low rub
  pickups       biro circle drawn around a note: rising two-tone +
                scratch (the sound literally draws the File 11 circle)
  UI            page flips (open/close menus), pencil tick on
                hover, a small satisfied scratch on confirm
  ambience beds wind (filtered noise, swells with File 05 gusts),
                distant birds every 20-60s, river surface loop
                near water with distance rolloff, bamboo clatter
                ticks in the grove when gusts peak

WHERE TO GET THEM FREE & LEGAL
------------------------------
  - Record your own (BEST for identity): phone mic + desk + paper
    + a biro. 30 minutes makes footsteps, UI, kills, picks. Your
    own desk IS your foley studio — nobody can clone it.
  - freesound.org — filter CC0; check license per file (CC0 = use
    freely; CC-BY = credit in an in-game screen)
  - Kenney.nl audio packs — CC0, game-ready, consistent volumes
  - OpenGameArt — mixed licenses, read carefully
  - Sonniss GDC bundles — pro quality, royalty-free, big files
  - jsfxr / ChipTone — synth SFX in browser for ticks/zaps
  - Music: search "lo-fi CC0 ambient" on frecensubecho/free labels;
    or generate ambient beds yourself: 2 chords + noise wind + a
    pencil-percussion loop at 70bpm = instant notebook mood.
  ALWAYS: keep an AUDIO_CREDITS.txt from day one.

IMPLEMENTATION (Web Audio, no libraries needed)
-----------------------------------------------
  - ONE AudioContext, created/resumed on FIRST user gesture
    (click/keydown) — browsers block autoplay; resume() in the
    click handler of your start screen.
  - THREE.AudioListener on the camera + THREE.PositionalAudio
    for world sounds (fire crackle at campfire, river, enemies'
    weapon sounds). PositionalAudio is INDEPENDENT of your custom
    renderer — your ink pipeline needs zero changes; audio just
    works with the scene graph.
  - Voice pool: max ~24 simultaneous; steal the oldest quiet voice.
  - Preload into AudioBuffers at load screen; play via
    BufferSource (no <audio> tags — no latency).
  - Distance model: linear rolloff, refDistance 3, maxDistance 60
    (fog ends at 110 — sounds should die before visuals do, or
    the paper world feels haunted).
  - Bus layout ( GainNodes ): master -> [sfx, ambience, music, ui];
    a 1-line mute each + settings sliders (File 11 Part 5).
  - Footsteps: no per-step events needed — drive from movement
    speed with a step-cycle timer; swap buffer set by ground ink
    (you KNOW the surface: trail decals = OR ink underfoot —
    query your own collider/decal data; no raycast needed if you
    track region by sector fields from File 03).

DYNAMIC LAYERS (the pro touch)
------------------------------
  - GUST SYNC: your wind gust function (File 05 U2) already knows
    when a gust rolls — scale ambience wind gain with the SAME
    value. World breathes in stereo.
  - COMBAT DUCKING: music/ambience gain -30% while enemies in
    combat state; back up on calm. 6 lines, huge feel.
  - LOW-HP TENSION: at low HP, thin the music (lowpass down) +
    faint page-rustle pulse — matches your red scribble vignette
    (uLowHp) so sight AND sound say the same thing.
  - THE TROUT SPLASH: sync a plip+ripple-crackle to the fish's
    landing frame (same phase variable as File 05's ripple ring —
    one event, two senses).

PERFORMANCE & POLISH RULES
--------------------------
  - Audio thread is cheap; the risks are: too many BufferSource
    starts per second (pool them), and unbounded looping nodes
    (always stop+disconnect on world unload — the map loader must
    own an audio manifest per level: fires, river, sector beds).
  - Persist mute/volumes in localStorage next to your graphics
    quality keys ('doodle_*' family).
  - iOS: context.sampleRate quirks + silent-switch — test once on
    a real iPhone early, not at launch.
  - The screenshot mode (File 06 U8) can't carry audio — but the
    shareable REPLAY clip idea can: even 5s of gameplay with the
    scratch-kill sound IS the trailer.
