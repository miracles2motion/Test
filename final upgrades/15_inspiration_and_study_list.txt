FILE 15 — INSPIRATION & STUDY LIST
The real Three.js work (and neighbors) worth studying — with WHAT
exactly to steal from each. Study = open DevTools, take notes,
rebuild one small piece, THEN move on.
=====================================================================

THE THREE.JS CANON
------------------
  threejs.org/examples — the official example zoo. Steal:
    webgl_postprocessing/* (compare your single-pass approach vs
    EffectComposer — you're a custom pipeline; know both),
    webgl_instancing_* (scatter perf patterns for File 10),
    webgl_buffergeometry_* (your hand-rolled tube/ribbon can
    borrow indexing patterns)
  three.js Editor (threejs.org/editor) — inspect scenes live;
  also a good sanity rig for testing imported geometry.
  Bruno Simon — bruno-simon.com (the portfolio that launched a
    thousand careers). Steal: how PHYSICS + charm > graphical
    fidelity; his Threejs Journey (paid) covers loops, shaders,
    performance — the closest thing to a canon textbook.
  Discover three.js (discoverthreejs.com) — free chapters on
    scenes, transforms, animation loops: solid fundamentals with
    real project structure (compare with File 07's layering).

SHADER EDUCATION (your pipeline's next level lives here)
--------------------------------------------------------
  The Book of Shaders (thebookofshaders.com) — pattern/noise
    fluency; your paper grain and future scribble jitter are
    chapter exercises in disguise.
  Inigo Quilez articles (iquilezles.org) — SDFs, distance
    functions, 2D shadertoy tricks. His 2D distance functions
    page can generate INK SHAPES procedurally (wobbly circles for
    splats without geometry).
  Shadertoy — search "pen", "sketch", "hatching", "crosshatch".
    Concrete study targets: how others do stroke noise and line
    wavering; compare their cost vs your world-space approach.
  Artem Posenenskiy... (skip obscure names — instead:)
  abstract/artsy three demos get posted weekly on r/threejs —
    save the best 10 in a folder; when stuck, ask "which saved
    demo is closest to my problem?"

PROCEDURAL GENERATION (Dream's field)
-------------------------------------
  r/proceduralgeneration + the procgen Discord/weekly threads —
    the community for your File 07-09 architecture
  "Polygonal Map Generation" (Red Blob Games, redblobgames.com) —
    THE classic interactive article; maps, Voronoi, noise. Read
    before any terrain/region work beyond sectors.
  Fast Poisson Disk Sampling (Bridson 2007 paper + web
    implementations) — upgrade your rejection sampling (File 08
    G1) when you need guaranteed-density scatter
  Simulated annealing / genetic algorithm primers — File 16 uses
    them as Dream's "no-LLM" optimization brain
  watabou's generators (watabou.github.io) — one-person procgen
    tools (villages, dungeons) with immaculate UX; study how a
    generator exposes KNOBS instead of options

GAME FEEL & LEVEL DESIGN (the craft layer)
------------------------------------------
  "Juice it or lose it" + "The art of screenshake" (YouTube,
    Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho / Jan Willem Nijman) — the two
    canonical 15-minute game-feel talks; File 11 is their spirit
    in ink form
  Level Design Workshop talks (GDC, YouTube channel) — start with
    "Level Design Workshop: Blockmesh and sightlines"
  "An Architectural Approach to Level Design" (Christopher W.
    Totten, book) — your tier/choke/beacon language, formalized
  Scott Rogers "Level Up!" (book) — the vocabulary book; cheap,
    fast, foundational

AUDIO (File 12's sources, repeated as reference)
------------------------------------------------
  freesound.org (CC0 filter), Kenney.nl packs, jsfxr/ChipTone
  Howler.js — if raw WebAudio ever fights you (you may not need it)
  SunVox/Bosca Ceoil/Reaper — free-ish music routes; or commission
    one 70bpm pencil-percussion loop from a jam musician for $30

TOOLS & LIBS TO KNOW ABOUT (borrow, don't adopt by default)
-----------------------------------------------------------
  GSAP — menu/title animation polish (SVG stroke writing)
  cannon-es / Rapier — physics, IF the game ever needs rigid
    bodies (your controller may not; measure first)
  pmndrs ecosystem (drei/postprocessing) — even if you stay
    vanilla three, reading their postprocessing lib teaches the
    EffectComposer idioms your custom pipeline parallels
  Ajv or Zod — recipe JSON schema validation (File 16, today)
  Spector.js — frame capture (File 10's microscope)
  Puppeteer/Playwright — headless screenshot CI (File 09)

THE STUDY METHOD (so this list doesn't become tab hoarding)
-----------------------------------------------------------
  1. One source at a time. 30-60 minutes, capped.
  2. Produce ONE artifact per session: a note, a fragment, a
     benchmark, or a shipped tweak. Reading without shipping
     doesn't count.
  3. Keep a STUDY.md: source, one insight, what you changed.
  4. Re-derive before copy-paste: rebuild the idea small, then
     adapt — your ink pipeline is custom; most borrowed code
     needs translation to it anyway.
  5. Rule of three: if an idea appears in 3+ independent sources
     (e.g., "budget before optimizing"), it's law — do it.
