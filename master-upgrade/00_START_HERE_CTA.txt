1. THE HONEST DIAGNOSIS (read this twice)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Your map is not "extremely terrible" because you lack skill, ambition,
or a good idea. It is terrible because of ONE structural mistake that
repeats every cycle:

   You are measuring your game with TEXT, not with your EYES.

Your "Dream" workflow produces documents (500-line concepts, research
dossiers, audit scores of "12/12" and "100% perfect") and then
congratulates itself. But none of those documents render. A map can
pass every audit you invented and still look like garbage — which is
exactly what happened.

The proof is in your own files:
  - research.md claims "12/12 PASS, 686 colliders, 47 grapples, 100%
    perfect" ... while you look at the map and call it terrible.
  - Those audits measure collider COUNT, stair COUNT, grapple COUNT.
    Not one of them measures what a screenshot looks like.

Meanwhile, the thing that would actually make a "ballpoint pen" game
beautiful — the ink line, the hatching, the paper, the light — does
not appear ONCE in your code. Not once.

Here is the hard truth, in one sentence:

   A ballpoint-pen art style is a SHADING + OUTLINE + PAPER problem,
   and you have written zero shading, zero outline, and zero paper.

You spent 100% of your effort on geometry (splines, lofting, Poisson
bamboo placement) and 0% on rendering. That is why every map looks
like flat plastic boxes no matter how clever the placement is.

The "terrible gaming loop" you describe is this:
   generate more geometry -> generate more docs -> pass fake audits
   -> map still ugly -> ask Dream to do it again -> more docs -> ...

You break the loop by changing the acceptance test from
"did the audits pass?" to "does the screenshot look like a pen
drawing?".

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE THREE PILLARS OF A "BALLPOINT PEN" LOOK (your north star)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Every decision you make from now on must serve these three pillars.
If a change doesn't serve one of them, don't do it.

PILLAR 1 — THE LINE IS THE ART.
   A pen drawing is defined by its OUTLINE. Every object, near or far,
   gets a thin black ink outline. No outline = not a pen drawing.
   (This is your single highest-impact change. Do it first.)

PILLAR 2 — SHADING IS HATCHING, NOT GRADIENT.
   Real ballpoint shading is done with parallel/crossed pen lines
   whose DENSITY encodes darkness. In a 3D game you fake this with
   cel/toon shading (hard 3-band light) + a hatch texture in the
   shadow band. Never smooth gradients.

PILLAR 3 — PAPER IS THE STAGE.
   Warm ivory paper background, paper-colored fog, a subtle grain
   overlay, and a limited ink palette (your 5 INK colors are already
   good — keep them).

4. HOW TO USE THESE FILES (read this part carefully)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The batches are ordered. Do them ONE AT A TIME, in order. After every
single batch you MUST do this ritual before starting the next:

   1. Run the game.
   2. Take a screenshot (PrintScreen, or renderer.domElement.toDataURL()).
   3. Put the screenshot next to your reference image.
   4. Ask ONE question: "Does it look MORE like a pen drawing than
      last batch?"  (yes / no)
   5. If no — fix that batch before moving on. Never skip ahead.

This ritual IS the game. The batches are just fuel for it.

Expected effort per batch: a few hours. Total: a week or two of
evenings to go from "terrible" to "genuinely pretty." You will see
the biggest jump after Batch 2 + 3 (toon shading + ink outline).
