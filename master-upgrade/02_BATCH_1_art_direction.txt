=====================================================================
BATCH 1 — ART DIRECTION: KILL THE DOCS, PICK A REFERENCE, WRITE 5 RULES
=====================================================================
Time: ~1 hour. No code. This is the most important non-code batch.

WHY THIS COMES FIRST:
You currently steer the whole project with text documents that never
render. You must replace that steering wheel with a PICTURE. Games
that "rival gaming companies" all start from a reference image and a
small set of unbreakable visual rules — not from 500-line specs.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 — PICK ONE REFERENCE IMAGE (30 minutes)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Find ONE ballpoint-pen illustration you love. Real pen-on-paper art.
Good search terms:
   "ballpoint pen illustration landscape"
   "ballpoint pen drawing architecture"
   "biro art" / "biro drawing"
   artists to look at: Alfred Basha, Mark Powell, Juan Francisco Casas
   (look at the STYLE, not the subject)

Pick one image. Save it to your project as  art/reference.png.

Rules for choosing it:
  - It must be the STYLE you want (pen lines, hatching, paper).
  - It must show the SHADING technique you want (crosshatch vs. contour
    lines vs. stippling). Notice which one you pick — you will copy it.
  - It should be a SCENE (not just a portrait), so you can copy how
    light and space are handled.

This image is now your north star. Every screenshot you take from now
on gets placed NEXT TO IT and compared. If your screenshot doesn't
echo it, you are not done.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 — WRITE YOUR 5 UNBREAKABLE VISUAL RULES (20 minutes)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Copy this template, fill in the blanks from your reference, and save
it as  art/visual-rules.txt. These 5 rules REPLACE forest_concept.md.

RULE 1 — THE OUTLINE (this is your style's heartbeat)
   Every object has a thin, black (near #111111) ink outline, roughly
   uniform width, slightly imperfect (a tiny wobble is GOOD — it reads
   as hand-drawn). Write down the exact color and feel from your ref.

RULE 2 — SHADING = HATCHING, NOT GRADIENT
   Light is represented by a small number of HARD bands (2 to 4), and
   the DARK band is drawn with pen lines (hatch/crosshatch), not with
   a smooth gray gradient. Write down: how many bands? which hatch
   direction? (e.g. "3 bands; shadow band = 45-degree diagonal hatch").

RULE 3 — PAPER IS THE STAGE
   Background = warm ivory paper. Fog = same paper color. A faint
   paper grain sits over the whole frame. Write your exact paper hex
   (your research already hints at #f5f2eb — confirm against the ref).

RULE 4 — LIMITED INK PALETTE + ONE LIGHT DIRECTION
   Keep your 5 INK colors (black, green, orange, blue, red) but every
   scene uses ONE consistent key-light direction (e.g. upper-left) so
   all shadows agree. Write down the light direction.

RULE 5 — SILHOUETTE FIRST
   Every object must be recognizable from 20 meters by its OUTLINE
   alone (no color, no detail). If you can't tell a pine from a stump
   by silhouette, redraw the shape. This rule kills the "box" look.

Keep this file under 1 page. If a rule can't be measured in a
screenshot, delete it — that's the whole point.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Move these into an  archive/  folder and stop running them as a

They are history, not quality control. Your NEW quality gate is:
