=====================================================================
BATCH 10 — CAMERA, COMPOSITION & DEPTH
=====================================================================
Time: ~2–3 hours. Rendering is fixed, shapes are fixed, it moves. The
last "why does it still look amateur" answer is usually COMPOSITION —
how the camera frames the scene and how depth is conveyed. This batch
fixes framing and leans hard on depth cues.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1 — FOV (this one number changes "feel" enormously)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Too-wide FOV = warped, cheap, "web demo" look. Too-narrow = cramped.
  - 45–55° for a grounded, painterly, "real camera" feel.
  - 60° if gameplay needs more peripheral awareness.
Pick one and stick with it. For a pen-art game, 50° is a sweet spot.
  camera.fov = 50; camera.updateProjectionMatrix();

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2 — DEPTH OF FIELD (optional but powerful)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
A subtle DoF (slight blur on far/near) is a HUGE "cinematic" cue and
hides low-detail distance. Your EffectComposer can add BokehPass.
  - Blur only the FAR field (the fog/mist region).
  - Keep focus on the player.
  - If it costs too much FPS, skip it — fog already does 80% of this.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3 — ATMOSPHERIC DEPTH STACK (do all four, they compound)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Depth cues, strongest first:
  (a) FOG (already in): near crisp, far paper-washed.  <- biggest
  (b) SCALE OVERLAP: foreground elements (grass, a trunk) partially
      cover the mid-ground — "framing" objects near the camera sell
      depth instantly. Add 1–2 large foreground silhouettes at screen
      edges (a dark trunk, a big leaf).
  (c) AERIAL PERSPECTIVE: distant objects desaturate slightly toward
      paper (your fog + grade already do this).
  (d) LIGHT SHAFTS: a few god-ray cones through the canopy = instant
      "forest light." Cheap fake: translucent light-shaft planes
      angled down from the canopy, additive, low opacity.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4 — FRAMING & THE "RULE OF THIRDS"
---------------------------------------------------------------------
When you place the camera or set up a spawn vantage, put the hero
object (treehouse) on a thirds intersection, not dead center.
  - Default spawn camera should look TOWARD a composed view: the
    treehouse framed between two trees, path leading to it.
  - Screenshot your spawn view. Does it look like a postcard? If not,
    move props/camera until it does.

This single habit (make every spawn view a postcard) will do more for
"gaming company" feel than any amount of geometry.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5 — COLOR SCRIPT CHECK (the last taste test)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Walk the map and check each sector's PALETTE still obeys your 5 inks +
paper. Kill any accidental 6th color or neon. Consistency of palette
IS the style.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
DEFINITION OF DONE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] FOV locked to a deliberate value (50° recommended).
[ ] DoF (if affordable) blurs only the far mist.
[ ] Foreground framing elements add depth.
[ ] (Optional) light shafts through canopy.
[ ] Every spawn view reads like a composed postcard.
[ ] Palette is 5 inks + paper, nothing else.
