=====================================================================
BATCH 11 — THE ITERATION LOOP (THE HABIT THAT KEEPS YOU GOOD)
=====================================================================
Time: 30 minutes to set up, then it's your forever-routine.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS:
Every ugly game is a game that stopped being LOOKED at. The people who
build "amazing things" with three.js don't have better tools than you —
they have a tighter loop: make a change, LOOK at it, judge, repeat.
This batch installs that loop so you never slide back into the
"generate docs, pass fake audits, map still ugly" treadmill.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE WEEKLY LOOP (do this every session, ~5 min each)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SCREENSHOT.  Load the game. Take 3 screenshots:
     - the spawn view (postcard test)
     - one mid-map vista
     - one close-up of a hero object (treehouse/fish)
2. COMPARE.  Put them next to art/reference.png and your 5 rules.
3. SCORE.  Rate each of the 3 pillars out of 5, honestly:
     - LINE  (outline present? uniform? hand-drawn feel?)  __/5
     - SHADE (hatching? hard bands? one light dir?)        __/5
     - PAPER (paper bg? grain? fog wash? palette?)         __/5
4. PICK ONE.  Choose the LOWEST-scoring pillar and ONE concrete fix.
   Write it down as a TODO. (One fix per session. Never more.)
5. DO IT.  Make the change. Then start the loop over.

That's it. Five minutes of LOOKING prevents a week of blind building.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ANTI-PATTERNS (catch yourself doing these and stop)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  X Adding MORE objects to fix a scene that looks empty.
       -> Instead: fix silhouettes, light, fog, framing first.
  X Writing/updating a concept doc instead of rendering.
       -> Docs don't render. One reference image > 500-line doc.
  X Trusting "audit passed / 12/12" as proof of quality.
       -> The only audit is your eyes on a screenshot.
  X Changing 5 things at once and not knowing what worked.
       -> ONE change per screenshot. Always.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE 10-MINUTE "COLD READ" (do this monthly)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Show the game to someone who has never seen it. Say nothing. Watch
their face for 10 seconds. Then ask ONE question: "What does it look
like it's made of?" 
  - If they say "pens / a drawing / paper" -> you're winning.
  - If they say "boxes / a game demo" -> you know exactly what pillar
    to fix next (LINE first, then SHADE, then PAPER).

This is worth 100 audits.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
SCALING UP LATER (once the look is locked)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Only after Pillars 1–3 are consistently 4/5 or 5/5, you can start
pushing toward "rivaling gaming companies" with:
  - instancing (InstancedMesh) for bamboo/grass/leaves — big perf win
  - a proper skybox or hand-drawn sky
  - particles (leaves falling, pollen motes in light shafts)
  - audio ambience (forest wind, river) — audio is half of "alive"
  - better enemy AI/animation (your research already sketches this)
But NONE of that matters until the base LOOK is right. Look first.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
FINAL WORDS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
You are not stuck because of three.js. You are stuck because you were
measuring a visual craft with text. You now have the three pillars
(LINE, SHADE, PAPER), the reference-image habit, and the one-change-
per-screenshot loop.

Go do Batch 1 tonight. Take a screenshot tomorrow. You will not be in
the "terrible gaming loop" anymore, because the loop you are in now
ends with a screenshot you actually like.

Good luck. Draw something worth looking at.
